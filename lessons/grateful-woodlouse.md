The operator asked for `stamp` to interview them when invoked without arguments. The primary wrote the interview procedure — its topics, order, question style and stopping rule — from general knowledge, recorded a scope that did not mention prior practice, self-checked against that scope, passed every gate and committed. The operator then asked whether best current practice for such interviews had been researched and incorporated. It had not.

The methodology route made this easy to miss. One-shot methodology work has no planner bibliography, no research budget and no reviewer to challenge an unresearched design; its self-check runs against a scope record the same primary wrote. Every gate measures the change's structure, and none of them can see that a procedure was invented instead of adopted. The template already does this well elsewhere: the `refactor` ruling required a methodology brief carrying the method with dated citations. The step was missing from the route, not from the template's habits.

The distinguishing property is whether the change **introduces a procedure** — an interview, a review method, a protocol, a decision rubric — rather than adjusting an existing one. An adjustment inherits whatever grounding the original had; a new procedure has none until someone supplies it.

**The rule candidate:** when authorized methodology work introduces a new procedure, research established practice before writing it, within the route's research limits, and record the sources with dates in the owning brief. Write the scope record to say so, so that the self-check can fail on an unresearched design.
