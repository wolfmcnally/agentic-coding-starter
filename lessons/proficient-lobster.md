Adopting a donor's rule imports the donor's **defaults**, not only its structure.
When the operator's ratified decision selects a variant the donor does not itself
hold — a looser trigger, a narrower scope, an exception the donor lacks — every
sentence of the port must be re-derived from *the decision*, not translated from
the donor's prose.

The failure is quiet and specific. The donor's text is coherent, so each carried
clause reads correctly on its own; the divergence only shows when the ported
clauses are read *together against the decision*. In the observed case, one
conditioning clause ("…and every named manual or subjective criterion is closed")
travelled into six surfaces and would have reinstated exactly the friction the
operator had declined, in a policy whose headline said the opposite.

**No gate can catch this.** A parity check compares mirrors, a catalog check
compares indexes, the full gate compares behavior — and all three are silent about
whether prose matches a decision made in conversation. The only instrument is a
deliberate read-back.

**The practice.** When a `learn` or `teach` decision selects against the donor's
own default, write the chosen rule down *in the decision's own words first*, then
port; and before the gate, re-read every touched surface against that written
statement rather than against the donor. Where the two would differ, the decision
wins by construction — that is what ratifying it meant.

Kin to `electric-goshawk` (a self-authored one-off arrives pre-trusted): there the
imported thing is a fact that was never a convention; here it is a *default* that
was a convention — in the other repository.
