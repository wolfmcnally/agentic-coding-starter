The plan had a section headed with what the change would cost the operator. It said there was no money and no recurring manual work. In the same plan, two bullets told the coder to run a survey before handing off every product change and told every sweep to run one across the whole estate. The step's duration was not known when the plan was written, and the plan said so, in a different section, as a reason to trial the tool first.

Both statements were true and they were never joined. A reader approving the plan saw a cost section with nothing recurring in it, and a use section whose recurring cost depended on a number nobody had yet. Once the number existed, five minutes per phase at the declared budget and hours for an estate, the instruction was already delivered, and it took the operator asking how often the thing would run to surface it.

**When a plan adds a step that runs at every phase, commit or sweep, its cost section states the recurring time, or states that the time is unknown and makes the step conditional until it is measured.** A step whose cost is unmeasured should arrive opt-in and be promoted to automatic by a later decision, not the other way round. The cheap check when writing a cost section: list every instruction the plan gives that fires more than once, and put a duration or the word unknown beside each.

Correction applied on the operator's direction the same day: the survey is opt-in, run through an operator-invoked skill.
