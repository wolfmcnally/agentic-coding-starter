## 2026-11-13 21:51 — END — METHODOLOGY — A timeout proof that could not fail is corrected and made independent of machine load

Scope: a recipient's 1.6.0 transfer stopped when one template test failed under machine load; the test was unchanged by the transfer and is the template's own. Correcting a template test at its source is within the standing direction to carry methodology fixes through this repository. No release: the operator ruled on 2026-11-13 that incremental changes ride the next actual release.

Found: `tests/test_kickoff_config.py::test_timeout_kills_descendant_after_group_leader_exits` had two defects. First, its hard clock of a fifth of a second also had to cover the stand-in's own start, so on a loaded machine the run ended before the descendant had recorded itself and the proof failed on its setup. Second, and worse, the stand-in recorded `$$` inside a subshell, which is the parent's process id, so the proof watched the leader, which exits by itself. With the watcher's final kill of the process group removed, the old proof still passed; that was observed, not inferred.

Changed: the stand-in's descendant is now its own shell and records its own process id; the leader waits for that record before it speaks and exits; only the idle clock is short, since it is the subject, and the start and hard clocks are generous; the assertion names its failure; and the proof kills its descendant if it fails, so a failing proof leaves nothing running. The proof was recorded as a modification with a recorded defect, observed failing with the final kill removed and passing on the original bytes.

Verification: the corrected proof passed repeatedly alone; the full gate ran on the final tree. Two processes left behind by the diagnostic runs with the planted defect were identified by their exact paths and removed.

Not done: no other timeout proof was audited for the same subshell mistake or for a clock that also gates its own setup; that audit is filed as a lesson. No recipient was changed.

Lessons:
- Filed in this change: a proof that records `$$` from a subshell watches the wrong process; audit the other stand-ins.
