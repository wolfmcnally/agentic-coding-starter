# Test-suite governance policy

Every executable proof belongs to the repository's declared proof estate. The manager, schema, reset procedure, lane integration, gates, hooks, transfer rules, and reassessment obligation travel together. Family identities, selectors, timings, witness receipts, risk judgments, dispositions, and survivors do not.

## Adoption and scope

Operator-ratified 2026-10-12. Apply admission and observed-red requirements when a test is created or modified. Existing suites are reviewed in bounded batches for a concrete reason, not as a prerequisite to adoption. Do not automatically rewrite, re-witness or delete the whole estate. Preserve completed resets, immutable baselines, historical receipts and append-only ledgers.

Freeze the recipient-local inventory of expanded executable leaves, families, gate and hook proofs. The manifest declares `adoption.mode: complete` when every baseline proof has a disposition, or `bounded` with a concrete reason and batch plan. Undispositioned baseline proofs remain executable and are reported as pending review; this is not an assertion of reviewed value. A later bounded batch adds its dispositions at their chronological positions without rewriting earlier evidence. Retiring a pending baseline proof also closes its pending review; once none remain, adoption can be marked complete without reordering the ledger. No deletion quota applies, including to newly stamped projects that retain every inherited proof.

Retain direct executable proof for every applicable custody, security, authority, concurrency, atomicity, corruption, recovery, public-contract, schema, deploy and core-success risk. An inapplicable class requires its rationale and activation trigger. An unwitnessed baseline is reported honestly; it is not retroactively deficient merely because no authoring receipt exists.

## Judging a proof

These criteria are the one home for proof-value judgment. Authoring, critique, reset, and reassessment apply them; persona and skill text cites this section rather than restating it.

**Failure mode and net value.** Operator-ratified 2026-10-31: every test must address a plausible anticipated or demonstrated failure mode and add confidence worth its authoring, execution and maintenance cost. Merely restating behavior true by inspection or enumerating every possible invariant does not earn a test. A test that repeats a plainly visible comparison without a credible failure risk adds no independent confidence; an actual security, protocol, boundary or contract risk can justify testing even a simple comparison. Name the risk and the additional confidence, rather than treating a category label, test count, coverage score or ability to turn red as sufficient value. Apply this judgment to new tests and to authorized bounded reassessment; it mandates neither exhaustive testing nor a suite-wide cull or deletion percentage. Existing safeguards, required checks and evidence-preserving retirement rules remain binding.

**Admission.** Before a proof is written or modified, answer these questions; a missing answer means it is not written yet:

1. What plausible anticipated or demonstrated failure mode does it cover, and what observable behavior or independent contract is at risk?
2. What credible regression makes it fail, and why is that failure a risk worth detecting rather than an invariant invented solely to test?
3. Why does existing coverage not already catch that failure? Each contract has one primary proof at its strongest boundary. Another layer needs its own distinct risk the primary cannot reach. Extending a table case or shared fixture is preferred over a near-duplicate proof.
4. Why is its additional confidence worth the authoring, execution and maintenance cost? Include attempted work transitively through fixtures, helpers, setup, teardown and child processes. Bound the relevant inputs, generated attempts, retries, polls and external operations concretely; name opaque work that cannot be counted.
5. Does it need a production seam (an export, flag, wrapper, parameter, or injection hook) that no production caller needs? If so, the proof moves to the real boundary and the seam is not added.

A proof that would break under behavior-preserving refactoring asserts the implementation, not the behavior, and is rewritten at the owning boundary before it lands. A regression proof must fail on the pre-fix code for the intended reason; one regression at the owning boundary covers the bug, not one per layer it crosses.

**Junk patterns.** A new proof matching one fails admission unless the retention bar names the contract it independently guards, and reassessment hunts existing proofs for them:

- assertion-free coverage probes, and assertions that cannot fail on any reachable input;
- self-comparisons and identity copiers;
- copied fixtures, inventories, manifests, or export lists;
- exact source, import, or string greps;
- private predicate or call-shape tests duplicated at a real boundary;
- duplicate invocations of one contract, including replays of a shared helper through each of its callers;
- proofs whose only purpose is preserving test-only exports, globals, or wrappers, and dead production code whose only callers are tests;
- expected values produced by the helper or renderer under test;
- mocks that implement the asserted behavior, or one identical mock standing in for different interfaces;
- fixtures that supply the receipt, ordering, or callback the code under test should produce, or persistence asserted against a store the path never writes;
- capability proofs that restate a declared flag instead of exercising what the flag promises;
- negative controls that pass for an unrelated reason, such as a refusal from a different guard or a rejection the production path never reaches;
- fault-injection and timing proofs that never show the fault or window was reached, such as a fake that raises without first asserting the state the real function would see, or a kill that can land between writes; each asserts the precondition and is shown red against a deliberately broken implementation;
- names or fixtures that promise more than the assertions check.

**Retention bar.** Resemblance to the implementation is a proxy, and it can invert: the proofs that guard exact bytes, keys, paths, and wire formats look most like the code they protect. Subject to the failure-mode and net-value requirement above, retain a proof when it independently enforces a public interface, protocol, configuration, storage format, security, platform, default, generated artifact, release, or architecture contract; when call order is observable behavior; when it is a regression with a credible failure mode; or when source inspection is the cheapest independent guard, failing when the contract changes and surviving an identifier-only rename. Static or slow alone is not a removal reason, and trivial-looking implementation alone does not waive a genuine risk. A retained proof that fails on the baseline is a suspected product defect: reproduce it and repair its owner rather than removing the proof.

## Removal and growth

Deleted and consolidated proofs MUST leave the executable estate completely. Their dead fixtures, helpers, and caller wiring leave with them. Skipping, deselecting, renaming, quarantining, or hiding a proof is not removal. A consolidated proof names a retained executable replacement; a deleted proof explains why it has no independent contract.

**Every retirement batch receives a recorded scenario-preservation assessment before it lands.** Methodology work follows the primary one-shot lane in [review lanes](review-lanes.md); the primary performs and records this comparison without inventing an independent review. Product work retains its selected review contract. The assessment compares each removed scenario with its actual keepers: input and preconditions, boundary, oracle, order/history/concurrency, lane availability, failure attribution and diagnostic value. A counterexample where the removed check fails and keepers pass defeats domination. Uncertainty means retain. Similar lines or coverage are leads, never proof of redundancy; no exhaustive all-pairs analysis is required. New or modified keeper assertions receive authoring evidence. Untouched keepers do not trigger retroactive witnessing. Each reported gap is restored or rejected with source evidence.

A new proof records an active contract or risk, independent oracle, observed red witness and specific marginal benefit in a `proof_admission`. A semantic scenario is the unit of judgment, not a function, assertion or family count. Expanded leaves remain inventory identifiers: adding cases or assertions inside an existing function still requires admission judgment. Review the diff for those additions; the inventory cannot detect them. Multiple assertions describing one result are reasonable when each can fail meaningfully and their failures remain attributable. Packing unrelated scenarios into one test does not reduce their review or work obligations.

Every created or modified test assertion requires recorded demonstration that it fails when its protected behavior is broken, including repairs to previously vacuous assertions and assertions carried into a changed keeper. Name which assertion each observation challenges; an early failing assertion does not establish that later assertions can fail. A targeted defect may support several claims only when the recorded observations establish each. No count, assertion, coverage, mutation-score or wall-clock quota substitutes for this judgment.

For a modified existing proof, append a `proof_modification` event with the repair/self-binding fields and one-based `witness_receipts` references to this modification's observations. References must name that proof and cannot be reused across modification events for the same proof. Historic `proof_repair` events and their receipts remain history. The selected review lane checks that references concern the actual changed assertions and candidate: structural validation cannot infer semantic modification or prove freshness from a proof id. An old receipt is never sufficient just because the identifier is unchanged. No standing replanting requirement applies to untouched tests.

A **red witness is observed, not asserted.** A mutant exists to vet a proof while that proof is being written. `./bin/test-governance witness begin` runs the named command and requires it to pass; the author plants the intended defect in the journaled files; `witness finish` requires the same command to complete with the declared test-failure exit (default 1) and the assertion-specific named text, restores the original bytes, verifies them, requires the command to pass again, and appends a receipt to the witness ledger. When the manifest declares `witness_failure_lines`, a regular expression matching the lines the test runner uses to report a failure, the named text must appear in one of those lines: a runner that echoes the failing source can otherwise print the message of an assertion that never ran. This template declares pytest's `E ` lines; a project whose runner has no such marker leaves the key out. The mutant is then discarded. A defect that leaves the command passing, or fails it for a different reason, is refused and nothing is recorded. The receipt carries the proofs, the named defect, the command, the expected text, the mutated paths, digests of the planted bytes and of the failing output, and the date. No red-witness patch is committed, and no close gate re-runs one. The ledger row's `red_witness` sentence states the claim; the receipt is the observation behind it.

The command works on the live tree with whatever command the repository's tests run under, so it is the same in every language and keeps the build cache warm. A warm cache is also the hazard: a cache keyed on a source file's size and whole-second modification time cannot tell a planted defect from the restored original when both were written in one second. The command therefore starts the planting in a later second than the files it journaled, and gives every restored file a modification time in a later second than the planted version. While a witness is pending, `validate` refuses, and with it the full gate and, in a checkout that has opted into the hooks, the commit hook: a planted defect cannot qualify a push, and cannot be committed where the hook is installed. `witness abort` restores the tree when a run is interrupted. A receipt is written by the tool, so it replaces a hopeful sentence with an observation; it is not tamper-proof, and review checks the underlying observation. A further challenge of untouched tests needs a separately authorized investigation; it is not routine authoring work.

Witness commands retain complete observed command/status/output JSON under ignored `.kickoff/witness/captures/` before their caller receives the result, including failed and restored executions. These are private evidence, not sanitized reports; preserve them with the run across reboot. No missing historical raw output is reconstructed from a digest.

**No mutant is stored, so the standing guarantee comes from the proofs themselves.** A committed mutant would re-prove on every run that its bound case still catches its fault, but a patch anchored on source lines breaks whenever the guarded function is edited, and when this template kept a corpus of them, three of its twelve held-out patches stopped applying within a week of being measured. Each critical risk's direct proof therefore exercises the refusal path on every run: it drives the guard with violating input, or disables the guard in-process, and asserts that the gate goes red. When a test is modified, the author demonstrates its assertions again; an unchanged committed test does not incur recurring witness work. (Operator ruling, 2026-10-06, replacing the 2026-09-29 corpus ruling.)

After the reset, retirement and repair are append-only events. A `proof_repair` targets one currently active proof, self-binds, carries the same evidence as a disposition, and leaves the active set unchanged; a proof may be repaired more than once.

A `proof_retirement` may target only one currently active baseline or admitted proof, exactly once. It records `consolidate` with a named active replacement or `delete` with no replacement, plus the same contract, oracle, red-witness, overlap, replacement, and rationale evidence as the original reset. Replay removes that proof. Renaming a proof is an admission of the new name followed by a consolidating retirement of the old one. The replayed active set MUST equal the live inventory; a shadow proof, repeated retirement, or missing event refuses.

## Mutation survey

A witness challenges one proof with one chosen defect. A mutation survey asks the opposite question in bulk: which small faults in this code would no proof notice? `./bin/mutate --changed-from <rev>` plants generated faults on the lines changed since `<rev>`, in a disposable copy of the candidate tree, runs the proofs that guard each file, and prints one document: its `state`, the tool, the scope, how many faults were generated, killed, survived, timed out and not run, and each survivor's path, line and change. `./bin/mutate --all` surveys every declared path, `--path` narrows either form to matching paths, and `--budget-seconds` overrides the declared budget for that run.

- **It is opt-in.** A survey costs time in proportion to the code it covers, minutes for a change and hours for an estate, so nothing runs one by default: no gate, no hook, no `kickoff` stage and no sweep step. The operator runs it when the work is consequential enough to justify the wait, through the `mutate` skill, which surveys changed code unless told to cover more. An explicitly approved plan that specifically names a survey counts as a request; generic approval to implement or strengthen testing does not. The request may cover new or committed tests, any subset and any duration. Defaults are conveniences, never authority or scope ceilings. (Operator ruling, 2026-10-12.)
- **It never gates.** The survey is not a member of `./bin/check all`, and there is no score to reach. A survivor is a work item, and some survivors are faults no observable behavior distinguishes. (Operator ruling, 2026-10-06.)
- **Three states, and failure is none of them.** `measured`; `partial` when the budget ran out, naming the files it did not finish; `unmeasured` only when the manifest declares no tool. Tests that fail before any fault is planted, or a tool that fails, exit non-zero and are never reported as unmeasured.
- **Declared per repository.** The manifest's `mutation` block names the tool, the path patterns in scope and `budget_seconds`, or `tool: null` with a `reason`. A derived project's paths name its product; the inherited methodology machinery is surveyed in the template. The interface and the per-language tool are specified in [`build-gates.md`](build-gates.md) § Language profiles.
- **A survey that was run is acted on.** When a phase's approved plan names a survey, each survivor in the requested scope is either killed by a stronger proof, which then gets its own witness receipt, or dispositioned in the implementation report as equivalent or outside any contract, and a reviewer treats an undispositioned one as a finding. A change that was never surveyed is not deficient for that reason.
- **The sweep offers, and records what was run.** A governed sweep puts a whole-estate survey in its decision queue with its expected cost and runs it only if the operator chooses. Every survey's dated counts and state go in the repository's history report, and survivors in code that guards a critical risk enter the decision queue.

## Attempted work, timing and hang safety

Ordinary wall-clock durations are diagnostic, on every machine. There are no per-test size ceilings or suite-duration pass/fail limits. Explicit product-performance requirements retain their own controlled performance tests; a timeout is not a performance assertion. `./bin/test-governance timing` reads the existing full-run record and reports runtime and slowest leaves. Missing, malformed or stale timing is unmeasured, never zero. Machine identity provides context, not permission to claim timings comparable. Do not rerun a completed slow suite to beat a threshold. Repeated diagnostic runs through the normal test entry point require a concrete question and explicit bounded scope; the manager has no sampling/rerun command. No automatic three-run confirmation or sweep reruns.

Bound **attempted work** where the cost occurs: attempted inputs (including rejects and shrinking where relevant), fixture expansions, helper subprocesses, retries, polling iterations and teardown. A bound on accepted examples does not bound generator invocations; a finite input count does not bound an opaque command's CPU or waiting. Choose the cheapest meaningful boundary, explain unavoidable expensive work, and document uncountable work honestly. Do not invent a universal weight or mandatory counter for cheap deterministic assertions. Limits apply transitively, so moving work into fixtures or packing cases into one function does not escape review.

The manifest's `hang_timeout_seconds` is a generous safety deadline, independent of ordinary timing reports. The Python profile supervises each pytest invocation with a monotonic whole-child deadline covering its setup, test bodies, teardown and inherited process group. Output cannot extend it. Governance child commands are separately supervised with a generous 1800-second deadline and a five-second termination grace, allowing the nested test supervisor to finish its half-second escalation and two-second output drain before outer escalation. Timeout or interruption returns 124 with `INCOMPLETE` and preserves available output; the gate cannot produce a passing receipt. A completed child failure remains a failure when its process group is gone. A remaining group makes the invocation incomplete even if its leader returned zero or a failure code: cleanup is unfinished, so that status cannot certify a pass or a red witness. Group-existence probes are conservative; a permission refusal or an unreaped group can produce this incomplete classification even when no live member remains. Preserve the output and diagnose this ambiguity; do not retry unchanged. An incomplete result establishes neither correctness nor a product defect. Witness tooling accepts only its explicitly declared completed test-failure code (`--failure-exit`, default 1, range 1–123) and the assertion-specific diagnostic. Use 101 for a Rust test failure or the documented gate/hook failure code. Reserved timeout, shell execution and signal codes cannot certify a witness. The receipt records the observed exit in its defect description; the author must distinguish a setup refusal from the intended assertion.

**Qualify the actual boundary.** Runtime selection and dependency provisioning before the Python supervisor starts are outside its guard. A descendant that deliberately leaves the process group must provide and test its own bounded cleanup; the group guard does not claim control over detached sessions. The supervisor itself launches the test tree in a new session/process group. Enclosing group termination reaches that tree through the supervisor's TERM, INT or HUP forwarding, not directly. SIGKILL of the supervisor cannot be handled and may leave the test group alive; enclosing tools must allow orderly cleanup or track and terminate both owned groups. They must also bound runtime startup and any independently detached groups. Demonstrate hangs, output chatter and resistant inherited children against the supervisor, and demonstrate that incomplete results cannot pass the gate or count as red witnesses. Do not infer this protection from a role watcher's existence.

An incomplete run may receive one bounded retry after a concrete condition changes, such as reduced contention. Record the condition and partial result. An unchanged retry or repeated same cause parks for diagnosis. Preserve the first result; never relabel a timeout as a correctness failure or successful check.

## Deterministic manager and lanes

The manager inventories expanded pytest leaves, collapsed families, gate members and hook commands; validates baseline/ledger replay, pending-review state, direct risks and witness structure; reports runtime; selects vital/changed lanes; records red observations; validates the mutation declaration; and reports the estate. Structural validation is not semantic admission review. Human/independent judgment supplies marginal value, domination and assertion sufficiency.

Changed-path selection is the local-commit and implementation-candidate gate. Invalid or indeterminate selection widens to full. The bare test run always executes the entire retained estate. The handoff gate, pre-push custody and durable full-gate receipts still answer for the entire estate, but the gate's test lane executes only the families that changes since the last fully passing tree can affect, by the same selection, and everything when that tree is unknown ([build gates](build-gates.md#bincheck)). A changed path classed as bookkeeping by the candidate partition is data like a document: it selects every family that covers or reads it instead of widening. A family of kind `command` declares proofs that belong to the deliverable's own test runner by the `bin/test` arguments that run them; such proofs are declared rather than collected, and their admission, witness and retirement rules are the same as any other proof's. Pre-commit validates structure, not full acceptance. Removing ordinary timing enforcement does not weaken correctness, custody or required full runs.

## Reassessment and transfer

A governed sweep reads `reassess` and existing timing reports, identifies pending baseline proof identifiers, grouping them by contract, and their age from available adoption/history records (unknown when no date exists), and chooses a bounded batch only for a concrete signal: changed behavior, a defect escape, confusing failures, observed cost growth or a known overlap. State the reason, boundary, attempted-work budget and stopping point before inspection. No forced shrinkage, automatic full-suite audit, mutation survey or timing resample applies. Review changed assertions and nearest relevant overlaps, not every pair in the estate.

At existing phase-close and sweep seams, report available runtime trends, added subprocess/wait work and unresolved cost concerns. Do not invent comparable baselines or rerun suites merely to obtain them. Many individually useful additions can still accumulate unacceptable cost; the owner decides whether to accept it, optimize a named boundary or authorize a controlled experiment. Diagnostic reporting does not promise automatic cost containment.

Transfer the machinery and these obligations atomically through `learn`, `teach`, `stamp` and bootstrap. Recipient-local selectors, timings, witness receipts, risk judgments, survivors and dispositions never transfer. Preserve any completed recipient reset and valid history. A new recipient may adopt with pending baseline review; do not require a whole-estate rewrite or a mutation-tool run before adoption. No recipient changes are implied by changing this template.

## Mechanical authoring

For the ordinary case, a new or changed proof, use `./bin/test-governance admit --input-json <file>`. Its `proofs` array gives, for each proof, the action (`admit` or `modify`), the proof id, the defect in words, the text the failure must name, the plant as an exact replacement (`path`, `replace`, `with`, where `replace` occurs exactly once), and the six substantive evidence fields; `command` and `failure_exit` are optional and default to running that proof through `./bin/test`. The command observes the proof pass, plants the defect, requires the named failure, restores and verifies the original bytes, appends the witness receipt, and only then appends the admission or modification row, computing every digest and receipt number itself. It records nothing for a proof it did not see fail. Before planting anything it checks that the finished ledger would be valid, so an input that could not be recorded refuses with nothing witnessed; the usual cause is another new proof the input leaves out, and the remedy is to give it every unadmitted proof at once. Each defect it plants is kept in `tests/proof-plants.json`, beside the tests it challenges. That file travels with the tests, unlike receipts and decisions: it is the input to a witness, not evidence of one. A receiver admits an arriving proof by naming it with its own evidence fields and leaving the defect out, and the command replays the recorded defect against the receiver's copy and observes the failure there. A recorded defect whose text no longer occurs exactly once has gone stale with the code; supply a fresh one, which replaces it. Planting happens once, when a proof is admitted or changed, never as part of a gate or test run. The separate `witness` and `author` commands below remain for what it does not cover: a defect that is not a single text replacement, family edits, retirement and disposition.

Use `./bin/test-governance author --input-json <semantic-input.json> --expect-manifest <sha256> --expect-ledger <sha256>` to serialize family edits and append evidence-backed decisions. Supply families (full semantic family declarations) and records arrays. Each record supplies action admit/modify/retire/disposition, proof_id and the six substantive evidence fields; modify also requires actual witness_receipts, while retire/disposition require the chosen disposition and replacement. The tool derives record types, baseline binding and admit/modify self-binding, validates the combined proposed estate through the ordinary validator, then publishes family metadata and appends records without rewriting the ledger prefix. This is not approval to cull or invent witnesses. Preflight refusals change neither file. A storage interruption between the two writes is an explicit incomplete operation requiring inspection of actual bytes; never report success or reset/rewrite history to conceal it. Ordinary gates continue to validate actual stored inputs.

One planted defect may witness several proofs only when all three hold: the witness command selects exactly those proofs and no others, every one of them is observed to fail on the defect and to pass on the baseline and the restored tree, and the defect is a fault each proof exists to catch rather than one that breaks the suite wholesale, such as a syntax error or a removed import. Record the shared defect on every proof it witnesses. A defect tailored to one proof is stronger evidence and remains the default for a proof written in the current turn; the shared form is for admitting many inherited proofs at once, as when a recipient catches up several releases (operator ruling, 2026-11-10).

## Required-check applicability and parallel execution

Before inheriting or retaining a required check, name its credible failure mode, independent oracle, nearest overlap, marginal confidence and transitive cost. Classify it as universal for an adopted methodology contract, conditional on a declared shipped capability, or template-only. A passing gate, category label or copied inventory is not a value assessment. Record uncertainty and retain an uncertain proof; do not claim its marginal value is established. Retirement removes executable code and dead support completely through the existing append-only lifecycle, with no quota.

Parallelize independent required tests by default within the receiving host's resources. A serial group needs a concrete shared-state, order or startup-cost reason; repair disposable fixture isolation before serializing unrelated tests. Use the ecosystem's maintained scheduler, not a bespoke parallel runner. Record the selected worker policy, nested subprocess cost and any exceptions. Keep whole-run supervision, faithful failures, complete identity/timing capture and cleanup. A crashed worker is not silently retried. A focused single-function diagnostic may run serially because process startup dominates it.

**Qualify a suite through the worker boundary, not beside it** (operator-graduated 2026-11-10). When a project adopts parallel execution, its existing suite is qualified by one full run through the actual parallel runner. A serial baseline cannot qualify it: nothing crosses a process boundary in a serial run, so it cannot show whether everything a test attaches to its result can make the trip back from a worker. Label subtests and parametrized cases with plain text, never with an object. In a donor, passing subtests whose display labels held an exception, a path and a malformed character each failed the run at the transport, one at a time over three days, with a diagnostic that named neither the test's subject nor its assertion. The same rule catches a different case: a custom exception raised inside a worker whose attributes cannot be serialized, which passes serially and aborts the parallel run when the failure it carries is reported. A failure of this kind is repaired at the label or payload, leaving the test's inputs and assertions alone, and is not a reason to serialize the test. No mechanical check for non-text labels is required; the one full parallel run at adoption is the qualification.

The Python wrapper uses locked pytest-xdist with load-group scheduling and no worker restart. Default parallelism is resource-bounded; explicit pytest worker arguments provide the diagnostic override. Nested wrapper invocations inside an xdist worker use zero workers, preventing recursive pools. Same-resource tests may use xdist_group; this serializes that group, not all unrelated work. Full/changed/focused lanes retain their complete selected estate and ordinary receipt semantics. Bounded calibration for an explicit optimization question is diagnostic evidence, never a recurring gate or permission to rerun until green.
